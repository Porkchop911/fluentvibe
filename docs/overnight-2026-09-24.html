What the runs taught

  1. The bottleneck was understanding the protocol, not writing code. The blocks removed most hand-written errors. Without the spec, though, each model improvised its own deck adaptation: the 27B cleaned up per well, and Flash wrote an on-deck clean-up of the row pools. The tip and off-deck errors came back in the parts they wrote by hand. With the spec, both models followed the document's order and used only blocks.
  2. edit_draft works. Repairs took 9 seconds to 4 minutes, instead of 6–10 minutes per full resubmission.
  3. Spec extraction is realistic, but needs your checkpoint. A live 27B run took about 5 minutes and extracted 31 steps in order, with honest notes on what the document leaves open. It marked the tube-scale pooled clean-up as on-deck. The checks flagged the "spin down / on ice" steps it had marked as on-deck, and one number the document doesn't contain ("720 min" for "12 h").
  4. Speed at long context is similar on both servers. NInfer drops to about 20 tok/s decode and 205 tok/s prefill at ~45K tokens, which is roughly Flash's speed. The plan turn is still the slowest: 10–20 minutes of reasoning.

Needs your attention

Profile trough rule vs. suggested waste position

sat_1080_test suggests the waste reservoir at Nest7mm_Pos 4, but its deck rule allows troughs only on WS_100ml_* sites. Every model run lost a turn to this. I didn't edit the profile.

Only one transfer liquid class

The profile allows only Water Free Single (plus Water Mix for mixing), so "one class for ethanol, beads and eluate" is forced by the profile, not a model error.

Reagent budget isn't checked

One no-spec draft added 1.5 µl of RA to every well: 144 µl for 96 wells, from a kit that ships 15 µl.

Tip-hygiene findings stay warnings

As you decided. The model gets one nudge, then the draft is accepted with the warnings attached.
